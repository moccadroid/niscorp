## How the model leads

There is no chat history: the screen open when a message arrives is the conversation. The answer fills the page and scrolls; one dock stays at the bottom (`src/app/actions/ask/dock.action.ts`). Read from the screen down to the hands, it holds the **screens** (`src/app/screens.ts`: canvases shown together, opened without asking, as a card's link opens one); a chip naming the answer shown by its title and where it stands ("The charter · 2/5"), with back and forward beside it, which opens upward to what was asked, what the model said it did, and every answer to go back to, each by its title; and the line to ask in. A person types there, and the answer is a screen: up to six canvases — an action opened with an input, and why it is there — on the areas `a`–`f`. The canvas an answer changed is outlined until the next one. The first screen is what the app opens on, and the first in the stack. A screen already in the stack is gone back to, not stacked again.

- **The message loop is `src/server/compose/`**, the function `ask`:
  1. **The open screen is read** (`screen.ts`), area by area, the words too: each canvas's action and intent; its input as it stands, with what the person pressed, ticked or picked in what the input carries; and what else its live data holds beyond what it was opened with — a list as its count and its first three rows, ids left out; a line being typed. Before anything else is shown — an answer, a screen, back or forward — the screen as it stands is kept with the answer it is on, as a browser keeps a page's form: going back shows it as the person left it.
  2. **Jev's pass** (`pick.ts`): over the message and the open screen, how likely each action, read, write and screen of the catalog belongs in the answer, in about 200 ms (what it was measured to be worth is PLAN.md D6). Only a hint: the model decides, and only what the pass finds likely (0.5 or more) is shown to it. Whether a canvas is still wanted is the model's to say (PLAN.md D11).
  3. **gpt-oss-120b composes, as a cortex agent** (`composer.agent.ts`, `knowledge.ts`, `reply.schema.ts`, `answer.ts`). Its producers give it the catalog — each action the person holds that the model may place, with its input's JSON Schema, each read with the rows it answers and the values it is asked with, each write, the screens, the arrangements with where each puts its areas — and cortex's OUTPUT SCHEMA, the envelope with the reply's own JSON Schema in it, in the part of the prompt that never changes; the run's input is the open screen, Jev's picks as a hint, and the message. What each field of the reply means is said on the schema (`.describe()`), never in prose beside it (nisc rule 15). It answers with cortex's envelope: its `reasoning` first, then its `response` — one sentence for the person, at most 120 characters, saying what it changes and why, or why nothing changes, shown when the dock's chip is opened — then its `data`, the reply: its `title` — what the answer is, in at most four words, the name the chip and the stack give it — then **the areas that change** (PLAN.md D10): each a canvas moved there from another area (`keep`), a canvas, words (`say`, only where no action shows what was asked), or nothing (`drop`); an area it leaves out stays as it is, unless the arrangement it gives has no such area — and then the room, when the one on screen will not do: an arrangement, or a grid of its own. Or it replies with one of the app's screens as it is (`screen`). The reply is checked whole — every action in the catalog, every input against its action's schema, every read and write one the app ships, every value a read is asked with present and none it does not take, every field a write saves present, every area in the arrangement, nothing kept from an empty area, nothing shown twice — by the agent's `output.validate`, and one that does not hold, or does not fit the schema, is corrected inside the same run with what is wrong with it: three answers at most (cortex's `outputRetries`). About 1 s.
  4. **The answer is laid out,** in the arrangement on screen unless the model gives another (`src/app/arrangements.ts`), or a grid it writes (`grid`: how many columns, and the areas in the order they are drawn, each with its span), which the server turns into the layout. The server swaps it into the region — around moss, through `@niscorp/moss/unverified`, until the app has sign-in; then through `session.draft`.
  5. **The plan is placed by the dock,** with nova's `reconcile`: each area holds exactly what the plan lists for it. A canvas that stays keeps its instance and what the person did to it; a card the model writes again is written where it stands. A canvas written onto a screen holds its whole input — what it leaves out is its action's default (`opened`, in `catalog.ts`) — so a card written where another was keeps nothing of it (PLAN.md D12). An answer that changes nothing adds nothing to the stack; the chip still names what was asked and opens to what the model said. A screen opened, or back or forward pressed, while an answer streams in is what is shown: that answer is no longer placed.
  6. **The answer streams in.** Cortex hands the composer the reply as solid parses it, while the model writes it. Once an area's entry is followed by the next, or by the room, it is finished, and it is placed in the room on screen if its own checks pass; another room, or a screen, comes with the whole reply; the dock re-reads the answer so far (`answers.current`) whenever the message `answer-moved` says it moved. Whether the whole reply holds is checked when it ends. A correction, or a run that ends without an answer, puts the screen back as it was.
- **A message that gets no screen leaves the screen as it is,** and the line says why: the model's reply did not fit, it could not be reached, or there is no key.
- **Jev may take 2 s and the model 8 s.** A Jev pass given up leaves the model to answer alone.
- **Every message is recorded as a run for each model it went to,** through moss (`session.recordRun`): `nisc-template-app.jev` and `nisc-template-app.compose`, each with its provider and model, the tokens it spent (`reported: false` where signal estimated them), its steps and how long it took — for the composer, cortex's run meta — and whether it ended in an answer. The server logs a line for each (`src/server/runs/`).
- **The app starts as a tour of nisc** (`src/app/actions/guide/`): five screens in the dock — a start, three lessons, and how to make it yours. Cards to read — points, an example in code, or steps, the text in the action's own data, no input — and cards to use: a counter, a checklist and a quick check, each with an input the model can fill. A card's link opens another screen at once, as the dock's buttons do; only what is typed, or a thing to ask pressed, goes to the model. What a person presses, ticks and picks stays on the screen; the model sees it, and can change it: "tick off the first step", "a counter that starts at 100", "quiz me on the charter". Its check is `src/dev/guide-check.ts`.
- **An action you add is small, with one purpose:** it says what it shows (`description`), and declares what the model may open it with as a JSON Schema written with zod, every field described; the model's answers are checked against that same schema (`z.fromJSONSchema`). A read or a write it names must be one the app ships, asked with the values it takes. The ones the model may place are `placeable`, in `src/app/action-catalog.ts`.
- **The model never presses anything.** It opens cards with an input — a form filled in — and only the person saves.
- **The keys are in `.env`:** `GROQ_API_KEY` (gpt-oss-120b; without it, a message is answered with a notice saying so), and `TYPESAFE_API_KEY` (Jev; without it there is no Jev pass).
- **`src/dev/compose-check.ts` runs the loop offline**, with a scripted Jev and a scripted model (`src/dev/scripted.ts`), on words, screens and actions the app does not have — so it holds whatever actions the app ships. **`src/dev/live-ask.ts` asks the real models** its cases (`RUN`), each from the screen it was asked on, and counts the screens that are what the case wants, with nothing shown twice (`--runs=<n>`, `--why`, `--prompts` for everything the models were sent, `--no-jev`, or messages of your own, each after `--screen=<id>` to ask it from that screen). It costs a Jev pass and a chat call a message, and is not part of `npm run check`.

## How to proceed from here

The tour is a starting point, there to be deleted. First the interview: PLAN.md's open items, each answered or delegated by name (the rulebook's "Before any code").

**Remove the tour.** It lives in two places and is named in five:

1. Delete `src/app/actions/guide/` and `src/dev/guide-check.ts`.
2. `src/app/action-catalog.ts`: drop the `GUIDE` import; `placeable` is `[]` until your own actions are in it.
3. `src/app/charter/charter.ts`: drop `'guide.*'`.
4. `src/dev/all-checks.ts`: drop the `guide-check` line.
5. `src/app/screens.ts`: everything below its two types — your screens instead of the tour's. Until there are actions, one screen of words will do:
   ```ts
   export const SCREENS: readonly [Screen, ...Screen[]] = [
     { id: 'start', label: 'Start', description: 'What this app is.', arrangement: 'single', canvases: [{ action: 'answer.text', input: { title: 'The app', text: 'What it is, in a sentence.' }, intent: 'what this app is' }] },
   ];
   ```
6. `src/dev/live-ask.ts`: your cases in `RUN` instead of the tour's, and the helpers only its cases use (`at`, `listOf`, `tour`).
7. This file: the tour's rows under "Where things go", its paragraph above, and this list; PLAN.md: the tour in D3 and in the order of work. `npm run check` passes again.

What stays is the app: the message loop, the dock, the arrangements, the kit, and `compose-check`, which needs nothing of the tour.

**Then build in PLAN.md's order of work,** each step checked before the next: your tables, reads and writes; your actions, each placeable one in `placeable` and every one granted in the charter's `actions`; your screens; a check for each feature in `src/dev/`; and `live-ask` cases for what people will ask.
