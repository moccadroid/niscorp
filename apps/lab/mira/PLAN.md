# PLAN — mira

Made 2026-10-20 by `npm create nisc`, on nisc 0.8.1. This is the record AGENTS.md's interview asks for ("Before any code"). The walkthrough asked what shapes the files; everything else is **open**, and an open decision blocks the build that depends on it until it is answered by the user or delegated by name. A blanket "go" delegates nothing.

## Decision points

| | Tier | |
|---|---|---|
| D1 Posture | answered | **A moss server app.** The shell runs on the server, one per person; the browser is a canvas terminal over a socket. The charter is enforced there: a principal reaches only the actions and the data it is granted. |
| D2 Environment | derived · **open for deployment** | **Postgres when `DATABASE_URL` is set** (`docker compose up -d` stands one up on this machine), else PGlite, an in-memory Postgres that starts empty every boot; the checks always run on PGlite (`src/server/runtime.ts`). The tables are `runtime.tables`: moss applies them at boot, and `nisc migrate` applies them as a deploy step. Open: where a deployment runs. |
| D3 Reads | answered | **Vex entries**, read by an action by its fingerprint (`/api/vex`), under the person's policy (`src/app/vex/`). |
| D4 Writes | answered | **Vex mutation entries**, posted to `/api/vex` by a form the person saves, never by the model. |
| D5 Routing | derived · **partly open** | The first screen is drawn on the server, for whoever the browser's session cookie says, and adopted by the terminal; a page drawn for somebody is theirs alone (`private, no-store`). The one route of the app's own is `/login`, where a mailed link is redeemed (`routes` in `nisc.config.ts`). Open: which paths are **pages** rather than the app, and whether the app's shell state syncs to the address bar. |
| D6 Who answers a message | answered | gpt-oss-120b on Groq composes the screen as a cortex agent (`GROQ_API_KEY`). The model may take 8 s; past that, the line says it could not be reached and the screen stays as it is. A fast picker (Jev, on TypeSafe) once read the message first and gave the model a hint: it was measured 30/32 right with it and 31/32 without, about 200 ms slower, and later it steered "what's on your screen?" to the Start screen in 7 of 8 runs, so it was taken out (2026-10-28). |
| D7 What a screen is made of | answered | Cards: each an action the model may place (`placeable`, in `src/app/action-catalog.ts`), opened with an input its schema takes — words are one of them, `answer.text`. Up to six, in order, on the one `screen` canvas; the screens in the dock are cards too (`src/app/screens.ts`). 2026-10-28: words were a field of the reply of their own (`say`), and each card said why it was there (`intent`), which nothing read but the prompt's own screen. |
| D8 History | answered | **A person's answers outlast the shell.** Every message they send is kept as a row of theirs (`answers`) with what was said back: an answer that put up a screen with that screen, one that left the screen as it was, or showed one of the app's screens, with the answer or screen it was asked on. A screen opened is not kept. A new shell (the first after the old one was dropped, a restart, a deploy) opens on the last answer that put up a screen, with up to 50 such before it behind the first screen, and the Conversation screen lists the last 100 messages. While a shell lives, an answer keeps what the person did on it when it is left, as a browser keeps a page's form; that is not kept in the row, so after a new shell an answer comes back as it was first shown. What the model is given of them is D15. |
| D9 Layout swaps | answered | **Through `session.draft`, where moss checks it.** An answer's layout of the `screen` canvas — its grid, an area for each card — goes in place of the canvas's own, under `canvas/screen`: one layout in the shell's draft, taken again for each answer, kept nowhere (`src/server/compose/index.ts`). Only somebody signed in asks, and every signed-in session has a draft. 2026-10-28: the answer was six canvases a–f laid out by an arrangement in the frame's `answers` region; it is one list canvas now, whose cards nova's own reconcile could not hold (one instance of an action per canvas, never reordered), placed by the app's `keyed` reconciler (nova `ShellConfig.reconcilers`, moss `NiscApp.reconcilers`). |
| D10 What keeps a card in place | answered | **The model answers with the screen after the answer, card by card in reading order.** A card on the screen is kept by its title, with what the person did to it, and keeps its instance wherever it moves: every card has a key, and a card kept has the key it had. A card given exactly as one on the screen is that card too. No cards leaves the screen as it is. No key reaches the model. |
| D11 Whether a card is still wanted | answered | **The model says,** by the cards it keeps. A fast picker asked it once was right 8 of 14 times on ten follow-ups, and with its answer the model dropped what was open. |
| D12 What a card written onto a screen keeps | derived | **Nothing of what was there.** A canvas the model or a screen writes holds its whole input: what it leaves out is its action's default, so a checklist written where another was shows none of its ticks, and a new quick check opens unpicked. A canvas an answer leaves alone keeps what the person did to it. |
| D13 Who gets in | answered | **Invite only, by a link mailed through Resend** (`RESEND_API_KEY`; without it the link is written to the server's log). The owner is the address in `MIRA_OWNER`, let in when the app starts; the owner invites everybody else from the People screen, and a person the owner invites is always a member. A link works once, within 15 minutes; a session lasts 30 days (`src/server/identity/`). Nobody signed in holds the sign-in card and nothing else. Everybody signed in reads their own row of `people` and changes only their own name (the `self` and `inviting` row rules); the owner reads every row. |
| D16 Your person | answered | **Mira is one person's assistant, and knows them.** The model is told about YOUR PERSON with each message: the name they want to be called, read as them from `people` (`people/me`), or that it does not know it yet and should ask; whether they own this Mira or were invited; and their time now, in the zone their device gives the dock (the server's until it has). A name they give is kept on the You card (`people.me`), which the model opens with it and they save, as every write is theirs. Asked 2026-10-21 not to touch memory: the name is a row of `people`, not memory, which comes later. |
| D14 Look and feel | answered | **Shingle Deck, in one scheme: Provenance.** An answer's cards are full-width layers, each one's rounded foot laid over the next, and the line to ask in is the last layer. Every answer to a message has an ink of its own, in turn (tangerine, pink, sky, lime, violet); a card keeps the ink of the answer that put it there, and no word on it says what the answer did; the line is in the ink of the answer it asks for next. Start is lemon and People mint. An answer moves down the page: a card kept slides to its new place, across columns too, a card's new skin wipes down over the old, a new card grows out from under the one above, one that goes folds away under it, and an answer laid out in other columns comes down over the old as one sheet; an answer still streaming in takes the move already running wherever it goes. Back and forward move along the stack sideways, as the overview lays it out: the answer shown slides out and the one it goes to slides in, and a swipe carries them with the finger. A new answer shows from its top, and back and forward return to where an answer was left. The dock is never part of a move — only its words travel: the question flies from the line into the chip, and the chip's words slide the way the move goes. Moves are drawn on copies of the cards over the page (`src/ui/target.ts`, `src/ui/motion/`), the look in the kit's stylesheet (`src/ui/kit.css`). The chip opens every answer side by side (`ask.deck`, on the `overview` canvas), the answer shrinking into its picture as a view transition; there the arrows, a swipe, a drag or the wheel move the pick, and Enter or a tap opens it. Start is a large card saying what Mira is, and things to try asking (`ask.suggestions`). An answer is one screen and never scrolls: cards that fit share it, and when they do not, every card but one folds to its title — the open one is the one the answer changed, or the one tapped, and scrolls inside itself; so six cards stays the limit, what a phone holds folded. The dock never changes shape. The answer is on the screen — one of the app's screens when one shows what was asked, an action, or words on a card — and what the model says back, addressed to the person and never a report of what changed, is kept in the Conversation, and under the chip only when the screen does not change. The first screen welcomes: an answer asked on it starts from an empty screen. The line grows with what is typed. Back and forward slide the screens a gap apart, rounded like the overview's pictures while they travel. The Conversation screen (paper-coloured) is a chat: every message and what was said back, the oldest first, opening at the newest; a message opens the answer it got, or the screen it was asked on. The overview draws each answer by its cards as they were drawn when the person last left it, folded as the screen folds them; swiping a picture up there, or its ×, closes the answer: it leaves the stack for good and stays in the Conversation. The other five schemes of the design rounds were left out, with the switch between them. |
| D15 Memory | answered | **The conversation, and nothing else.** With each message the model is given the person's last ten messages and what it replied, the oldest first — from this shell and before it, those that left the screen as it was too — as the chat itself, each message followed by what was said back, beside the screen; it may follow on from either. Nothing else is kept of a person between messages. The model is also told what Mira is: how many cards a screen holds, the stack, the dock (`ABOUT MIRA`, in `knowledge.ts`); on eight runs of three questions about Mira it answered all of them right. |

## Answered when it was made

- **Who leads:** a model. A message is answered with a screen: gpt-oss-120b composes it from the app's actions and the screen as the person sees it, and the dock places it on the one screen canvas, its cards matched by key. The keys are in `.env`.
- **What draws the screen:** React. The kit in `src/ui/` is where the look lives; a layout names components, never styles.

## The dial-in — open

- The entities, and how they relate — and what a person will ask about them.
- Expected scale.
- Who uses it: one person, or roles. More than one principal sketches its roles and charter now, not later.
- Where it is deployed.
- What happens to the data long-term.

## Order of work

1. **The interview** — every open item above, answered or delegated by name. ← here
2. **Scaffold** — done: the frame, the message loop, the dock, and their checks.
3. **Data** — per D2/D3, your tables in `src/db/schema.ts`, your reads in `src/app/vex/`.
4. **Actions** — your own, small: one purpose each, a description that says what it shows, an `input` schema for what the model fills in; the ones the model may place in `placeable`, each granted in the charter.
5. **Screens** — the ones a person will want in the dock, each its canvases; the first the one the app opens on.
6. **Writes** — per D4; a form an answer opens, filled in by the model, saved by the person.
7. **Checks** — a check for each feature in `src/dev/`, beside `compose-check.ts`, which runs the loop offline.
